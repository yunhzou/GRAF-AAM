\subsection{Search and comparison protocols}

The active-edge floor is $b=0.2$ and the matching tolerance is $\tau_{\mathrm{iso}}=1.0$. Seed streams are deterministic with root seed 42. Sweeps retain the uncut search and individual source-edge deletions. Bidirectional Golden output is a union of returned relations; branches from opposite directions are not spliced. Growth and branch caps bound compressed candidates rather than explicit mapping counts.

Golden uses dataset revision \texttt{793475e}. Its heavy-atom evaluator compares correspondence relations modulo endpoint chemical symmetry, retains unmatched atoms, and rejects altered endpoint chemistry. Hydrogen atoms participate in search but their individual identities are not reference labels. All 1,851 records remain in the denominator. All reported configurations were rerun against the final algorithm source, commit \texttt{3a9ef9a}. Execution manifests and component hashes accompany the per-case results. Golden evaluates the fragment-search stage, while the coordinate protocol also evaluates competition and event decoding.

Each search or evaluation subprocess has a 300-second watchdog and an initial 3 GiB sampled-memory limit. Externally interrupted phases can resume their completed cuts for at most two bounded continuations with a 6 GiB allowance. Golden reference evaluation has a 220-second internal budget. Completed raw cuts can also be finalized and verified individually, without new search. A positive result requires a verified witness; it can establish recovery even when another direction or the combined archive is incomplete. A negative result requires completed verification of the saved output; other cases remain unknown.

The campaign begins on an Apple M4 Pro Mac with 14 logical CPUs and 48 GiB RAM, using at most eight single-core workers, and continues on Linux CPU nodes with a final concurrency limit of 150 single-thread workers. Python 3.12.14, RDKit 2026.3.6, pynauty 2.8.8.1, SymPy 1.14.0, and Z3 5.1.0.0 are fixed across hosts. Native extensions are rebuilt on Linux; source-hash checks, focused tests, and two search/reference parity cases verify the port. Numerical-library threads are fixed to one. CPU averages use \CommonCases{} common reactions with both one- and two-order searches completed on the Mac and complete comparator output. Search-call timing includes checkpoint I/O and excludes imports, preparation, reference evaluation, and interrupted work; SLAP's instrumented workflow excludes I/O. Higher-order searches use mixed hosts and are excluded from this timing comparison.

The SLAP comparator uses SLAPMapper 1.0.0, revision \texttt{ea248fd}, with both directions and binary and weighted modes. Each sweep variant uses one original ordering and rebuilds initialization for the uncut input and each single-edge deletion. Returned labels are restored to the original endpoint structures before evaluation. Its \SlapRecovered{} verified Golden recoveries leave \SlapAbsent{} completed misses and \SlapUnknown{} unresolved records; all remain in the denominator. The sweep perturbs SLAP's input graph and assignment objective, whereas GRAFT relaxes fragment-preservation constraints.

On the coordinate collection, the exact original XYZ-derived SLAP adjacency matrices are restored from the saved graph records and verified against every source-edge deletion. Atom order and atomic numbers are retained; mapping labels are reset. Fresh native and bidirectional single-edge SLAP runs use binary graphs and the original initialization. The XYZ-to-graph preparation itself is not rerun. GRAFT uses the cached WBO matrices. Each returned SLAP heavy-atom assignment receives a symbolic hydrogen refinement under the comparator's original bond-change objective, and its witness is rescored on the original WBO matrices using \Cref{eq:events}. Tied hydrogen optima can give different signed-event classes; the reported comparator set comprises the returned witnesses, not the entire hydrogen label relation. This compares the stated endpoint workflows rather than isolated solvers on identical graph representations.



\subsection{Bounded fragment competition}

The coordinate configuration uses one seed ordering, cap 2,000, up to eight lowest-event distinct parent source partitions, at most two successive priority changes, and at most 128 proposal-growth calls and 128 completion calls per reaction. Smaller challenger fragments are visited first, with deterministic contact order. A proposed challenger is rematched with a single contacting source atom. Conflicting source assignments and occupied target images are released; attached components of at most eight atoms can also be released around the affected region. Remaining assignments are anchors for completion.

The admission check evaluates every complete representative against the original endpoint weights and its required fragment constraints. A completion graph is excluded if any checked complete representative violates these constraints. Only accepted graphs contribute to reported coverage. The ordinary search output is retained in union. Pending queue entries, omitted parent partitions, and capped continuations are not covered by decoding certificates.

Competition and decoding use five-minute per-case watchdogs with persisted results. Complete family certificates survive continuation, and unfinished families remain explicit. The full coordinate search, competition, and decoder stages are rerun from the original endpoint inputs. Timing for complete final decoding includes all of its bounded passes; it excludes the preceding search and competition stages.

\subsection{Lossless flattening}

For each complete search path, flattening retains its terminal representative, the union of required fragment-bond constraints, and the allowed action sequence with prefix locks already applied. It removes only representation choices that leave the admissible relation unchanged. Reordering disjoint-support factors preserves their product; adjacent nested factors satisfy $HG=GH=G$ when $H\leq G$. Subgroup containment is checked by exact generator membership using a stabilizer chain. Other factors with overlapping support retain their order. Identical resulting records are shared, and final branch grouping stores the union of their family identifiers. Consequently, the union of mappings before and after grouping is the same. The procedure does not infer equality merely from a shared fragment partition or a shared automorphism group.

The event decoder obtains completeness by invariance certificates, valid lower bounds, or an unsatisfiability result after excluding every discovered output class. Budget exhaustion is recorded as incomplete. This distinguishes preservation of a saved mapping union, exhaustive decoding of a specified event window, and exhaustive atom-mapping search.
