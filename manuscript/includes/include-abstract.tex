Atom-to-atom mapping for reaction analysis requires retaining alternative correspondences without enumerating redundant atom permutations. We introduce GRAFT, a graph-based method that grows matched fragments on weighted reactant and product graphs, represents compatible placements through correlated symmetry actions, and branches both on saturated placements and on competition between fragments. The default pipeline uses one seed ordering per cut, the cut sweep, and fragment competition. Completed searches are deduplicated by unordered matched fragment pairs while preserving their internal mapping alternatives. A symbolic decoder extracts distinct signed bond-event patterns directly from these families, separating the tolerance used for matching from the thresholds used to interpret bond changes. On all 1,851 Golden benchmark records, the bidirectional sweep-search stage, before competition, recovers an annotated correspondence among its retained alternatives in \GoldenOneRecovered{} cases (\GoldenOnePercent\%) with one seed ordering per cut (default) and \GoldenTenRecovered{} (\GoldenTenPercent\%) with ten. All GRAFT reference-recovery verdicts are resolved. The strongest evaluated prior-method alternative-coverage baseline, the bidirectional SLAP sweep, recovers 97.03\%. On the matched 1,821-reaction timing cohort, the one-seed GRAFT search stage and SLAP sweep average 2.52 and 4.82 CPU seconds per reaction, respectively. On 140 coordinate/Wiberg-bond-order reaction pairs, GRAFT reaches the same minimum event count as the SLAP sweep in 136 reactions and fewer events in four. Where their minima agree, it retains 162 of 164 comparator minimum-event patterns. This bond-event comparison uses unverified mappings and does not measure mapping accuracy. GRAFT provides an inspectable representation of mapping and bond-event alternatives for subsequent chemical assessment.
