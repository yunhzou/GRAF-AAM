Atom-to-atom mapping for reaction analysis requires retaining alternative correspondences without enumerating redundant atom permutations. We introduce GRAFT, a graph-based method that grows matched fragments on weighted reactant and product graphs, represents compatible placements through correlated symmetry actions, and branches both on saturated placements and on competition between fragments. Completed searches are deduplicated by unordered matched fragment pairs while preserving their internal mapping alternatives. A symbolic decoder extracts distinct signed bond-event patterns directly from these families, separating the tolerance used for matching from the thresholds used to interpret bond changes. On all 1,851 Golden benchmark records, bidirectional search with edge sweeps recovers an annotated correspondence among its retained alternatives in \GoldenOneRecovered{} cases (\GoldenOnePercent\%) with one seed ordering per cut (default) and \GoldenTenRecovered{} (\GoldenTenPercent\%) with ten. All reference-recovery verdicts are resolved. On 140 coordinate/Wiberg-bond-order reaction pairs, the configuration with fragment competition recovers \SweepRecovered{} of \SweepClasses{} minimum-event classes among the returned SLAP-sweep witnesses, with complete comparator-class coverage in 139 reactions. Final fragment deduplication reduces 237,645 ordered branch records to 124,641 unordered combinations. GRAFT provides an inspectable representation of mapping and bond-event alternatives for subsequent chemical assessment.
